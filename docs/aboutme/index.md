#### What does it mean to defend an engineering decision: and do you currently know how to do it?
<p>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To defend an engineering decision is to know that what you designed will both meet and exceed the requirements of a project based on your research, experience, and knowledge, while also being able to admit fault if something were to go wrong with your design. As an engineer, you must be able to reliably, and confidently, solve problems. These problems may come with seemingly impossible asks, however, as engineers it is our job to make the impossible possible. However, sometimes the impossible is impossible. You may think using a certain alloy will dramatically improve performance while reducing cost, but then come to discover that the allow had a strange property you were unaware of that causes it to fail in your intended application. As such as an engineer you must admit that your knowledge at the time was limited, BUT that you will take this opportunity to grow and become more knowledgeable, and share that knowledge unto others.</p>
<p>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;As of now, I am unsure if I have the technical ability to be able to design a complicated product that meets and exceeds certain requirements due to my lack of experience, and I am unsure if I am able to admit that something I thought was a perfect design, turned out to be a total flop, as I have done so in the past. However, as I continue my path as an engineer, I plan to take every opportunity I can to grow, improve, and become more knowledgeable in the field of engineering so that I may be of use to my brothers and sisters on this planet. </p>
